# Native iOS design contract

Reference: the user's Claude artifact, 14 artboards, inspected on 2026-10-18. This records the intended world; implementation and verification status are tracked separately.

- One continuous, edge-to-edge photo grid. At the top of the feed its first row starts half a photo down, under the fading edge of a soft blur behind the status bar; scrolled photos pass under the blur. Four columns by default, fine separators, and the month as a small glass chip in monospaced capitals on its first photo. No month heading is drawn over the feed; the rail's lens shows the month while scrubbing, and VoiceOver finds it at the top as a heading. The chip is dark tinted glass so it stays legible over white photos. Videos show their length as bare monospaced text with a shadow; tiles carry no cloud or phone storage badges. Only state that must stay visible floats at the top: the active filter shelf, album uploads, search checking and, while selecting, the month menu and Done. Selection starts with a long press on a photo; the account lives in Settings. Floating chips push the first row below them so short result lists are never hidden.
- Every photo cell shows something at once: a thumbnail still in memory, the same photo at another size, or its thumbhash sketch, then the stored thumbnail, then the download, crossfading. Downloads wait while photos pass faster than they could arrive and start from the middle of the screen when the grid slows; a download that has started is kept even if its cell scrolls away. Thumbnails are stored by photo and revision, not address, and the whole library is kept on disk on unmetered networks while the app is open (about 12 KB a photo). Pinching changes density during the gesture, step by step, around the photo under the fingers.
- A glass search capsule sits above the bottom safe area. Search and people/album/place/media filters update the same persistent photo feed. Lightroom shows every photo with a developed preview, laid out by month as a person's photos are. The unfiltered scroll position survives clearing a filter. Browsing never navigates to a second photo grid.
- Left-edge shelves fan out as native glass capsules over the library and work as one swiper under one finger, so the thumb never has to lift until it has found what it wants. A slight inward swipe anywhere along the edge pops them out at full size with a row under the finger: the current choice if there is one, otherwise a row about a quarter of the way into what fits on screen, so there are always shelves above the finger too. There is no stretching with the drag, and no pop without an inward move. Sliding up and down highlights the row under the finger; the current row holds a little past its edge and the wave leans gently toward its center, a light detent with no snap. A push toward the tips that goes more sideways than up or down, along a row's outer part, takes hold of that row: its selection starts at once and keeps building while the thumb drifts up to a row away, and the wave stays on it while a scrolling list waits. Pulling back or drifting further lets go. Only the row under the finger grows, to about 65 points, smoothly with the finger as the widths do, and its neighbors make room so its gaps stay clear. A push toward a row's tip gathers a blue glow there: it keeps building while the finger stays anywhere along the row's outer half, slowly then faster and quicker nearer the tip (a third to two thirds of a second), falls away completely when the finger moves back, and opens the row at its peak. Right after a level opens under the finger, its row waits for the finger to come back before it can glow. Rows are short enough that the tip is well within the thumb's reach. A group (People, Albums, More, Settings) opens in place under the finger with whatever is under the finger already highlighted, so the same stroke keeps scrubbing the new level. Swiping the left edge goes back a level inside a group; at the top level, bringing the finger back over the edge folds the fan. Lifting the finger anywhere puts the shelves away: the rows slide back into the edge. When a person or album was chosen on the way, the whole column instead scrolls up until that row sits where the filter tag lives at the top, while the other rows slide out completely, and the row becomes the tag: on a soft spring it shrinks to the tag's shape while its face slides to the front and its title follows, and the tag takes over in place with its clear button fading in beside it. Past a row's right edge the row stays focused but its activation ends. In People and Albums a round reorder button, as tall as the focused row, waits just past its tip and fades in as the finger nears it; lifting the finger over it (anywhere within a row's height of it) or tapping it reorders them in place: every row takes one width with a drag handle inside it on the edge side, the list scrolls like any list (it follows the finger, coasts and slows after a flick, stretches past either end and springs back, and a touch stops it), a row carried by its handle follows the finger while the others make room, a long list scrolls by itself while a carried row is held near the top or bottom, and Done at the top right finishes and puts the shelves away (the left edge finishes too). There is no close button: lifting the finger puts the shelves away, as does tapping beside the rows, and VoiceOver's escape gesture does too. The order is kept on the phone for each account, and people or albums it does not know yet follow the ordered ones. Choosing and scrolling are separate: the rows stand still under the finger, so it moves one row per row height at the top of a long list and at its bottom alike. A list that does not fit scrolls only while the finger stays near the top or bottom of the rows, from half a row inside the outermost one: slowly at first, so a passing row can still be caught, and faster the further out the finger goes. The rows passing under the finger are highlighted in turn; past the outermost row, the highlight and the wave stay on the row coming into view. Moving back toward the middle stops it, and a list opened under a finger already there waits until the finger goes further. The row under the finger has the widest local bump, neighboring rows soften it, and unaffected rows keep one width and a shared left anchor. Each row is a glass capsule growing from the screen edge, with no connecting rail. Rows near the finger stand apart with clear gaps, while outer rows grow into their gaps so their glass merges into one continuous wave along the edge. The Albums shelf ends with New album, which opens the album editor, above Manage albums. Faces on the shelves have their own memory cache and finish loading after their row scrolls away, so a fast scrub never leaves blank faces. A normal tap on the edge cue does not open the menu. Choosing a person, album or other filter applies it to the feed at once, while the finger stays down, so another can be tried before lifting. The filter tag stays hidden until the finger lifts and the chosen row has risen into its place, so the row never lands on a copy of itself; the row already selected never activates. The choice stays at the top as a shelf, with a separate round button beside it to clear it; dragging or tapping the shelf brings back its shelves with the current choice under the finger. While the shelves are out the tag and its buttons are hidden completely, glass included. An album in the feed has a share button at the top right, across from its title, to create a link or share it with people. Its Sharing screen lists the album's members and the links that share it, so an album shared only by link shows as shared there. Rows still respond to taps, and VoiceOver reaches reordering and Move up and Move down as row actions. The Settings shelf has a row for every settings screen (Account, Backup, Read-only mode, Appearance, Grid, Network, Notifications, Server, Diagnostics, About) and a Sign out row that asks first. Editing, management and settings use native secondary screens. Searches of the library, a person or an album never include the Locked Folder, even while it is unlocked, and widgets show only timeline photos.
- The right time rail is a faint tape scaled by photo count, with a short tick per month and a longer one per year, always visible; with the Earth open it runs above it and still scrubs. Scrolling slides a glass puck with arrows around a glowing accent line out of the screen edge as its glass forms; it slides back 800 ms after the grid stops. Touching the rail springs the lens and a glass column with the years out of it first. While they open, the lens shows the month under the finger and the photos stay where they are, so nothing moves before you can see where it goes; lifting that soon moves nothing. Once they are open, the photos go to the month under the finger, and dragging moves the grid continuously at the finger's pace, and they stay fully open, unchanged by how the finger drags; lifting slides them back into the rail sooner and quicker than the puck leaves after scrolling. Where the finger stops, the photos stay: once it has rested for about a third of a second, a move within about four millimeters reaches the photos only after the finger has stayed down a further 0.15 s, so the roll of a lifting finger never moves them, while a larger move is followed at once. The column shows the years, the current year bright and a chosen year on an accent pill. It also shows trips: months mostly abroad, named by city or country and kept clear of the lens. The lens shows the month, places, count and four photos, joined to the column by a small glass bridge. A month is named by its places even before its photos load, always by the places of the feed shown, so clearing a person or album brings the library's places straight back, and the places kept on the phone show straight after launch while they are counted again. An accent line marks exactly where you are: under the finger while scrubbing, at the grid's own position otherwise. The rail's top is always the newest month and its bottom the oldest; the month shown and the months that load follow the photos actually on screen, even right after a jump. The grid darkens toward the rail. One haptic tick per month. Reduced Motion removes travel animation.
- Haptics use one vocabulary: a light tick when moving between items (shelf rows, months), a firmer tap when the shelves or a group open and a soft one when they close, a crisp tap when an item or year is chosen, a light tap when the rail is taken, a rumble that rises with the glow at a shelf row's tip and gives way to one crisp click when the row opens, a rising run of ticks that lands on a crisp click when rows come loose to be reordered, a soft tap when a reordered row is dropped, and a longer rumble that swells as the Earth rises and fades as it sinks. All follow the haptics setting.
- Places replaces bottom search with a partially visible 3D Earth rising from the bottom edge. It is always the schematic night Earth of the reference: deep indigo sea, muted violet land drawn as vector geometry from Natural Earth, so coastlines stay sharp at every zoom, rendered at the screen's full resolution. A cool moon from the upper left shades it as a ball, a haze thickens toward its edge, and a thin blue rim of air surrounds it. Major highways glow faintly on the land. The user's photos glow on it like city lights, brighter and larger where there are more of them, merging from afar and separating as the camera comes down; very faint strings join neighboring lights, bowing sideways and rising slightly off the ground, never ruled straight lines. Selecting a real location turns the Earth to it at the current zoom and filters the same visible feed while the Earth remains open, laid out by month as a person's photos are, so the rail, the months' places and the trips stay (until the offline library has synced, the place's photos are found by search instead); rotate and pinch stay interactive, and pinch reaches about 20 km above the ground. The handle sits above the Earth's edge at any zoom and closes it only with a swipe down that the panel follows. Photo-location actions return to this feed and focused Earth. No invented trips, place names, counts, or markers.
- Search results present actual verification state and useful recovery. Preserve the reference's filter chips only where backed by a real server field. Do not invent an understood-query breakdown or progress percentage.
- The app stays upright; only the photo and video viewer turns with the phone, keeping the same photo on screen, and the app turns back upright when the viewer closes. Viewer presents the photo on black with sparse glass controls, a dismiss gesture, zoom, paging, video/Live Photo playback and a native details sheet. The viewer's round buttons, Close among them, are the same 48-point glass circle as its actions menu. A video has a mute button among them, as in Photos; muting carries on to the next video and matches the setting. A video's sound plays even with the phone set to silent, as in Photos, pausing other audio until the video is left; muted videos and Live Photo motion mix with other audio and follow the silent setting. Paging slides photos side by side with a black gap between them, as in Photos; the neighbors load ahead, and a photo opens on its cached preview or its grid thumbnail while a slow preview loads. Details include real EXIF, albums, OCR, and Lightroom development state.
- Use system typography and SF Symbols for native Dynamic Type and accessibility. Semantic foreground/background colors adapt to appearance. The reference's cyan selection accent is reserved for selected controls; the app's star identity is retained.
- Use actual native Liquid Glass on iOS 27 and shared glass containers. No CSS-style imitation of refraction, no full-grid blur while a search runs. Press and gesture feedback remains interruptible.

The downloaded HTML reference is evidence only, never embedded into the app. Its editor-only glow rule is excluded from the local design detector; native UI remains subject to visual review.
